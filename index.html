ECHO SHIFT

Every move you make is replayed N turns later by an echo of yourself. Echoes are solid: they block you, they hold down pressure plates, and if one steps into you, that is a paradox. You must move every turn — there is no waiting, so nobody can hold a plate for two turns running and a gate never stays open. It blinks.

Two things bend that. Shards seal the exit until you have collected every one, and each one you hold is a point of power — which is the only thing that beats a sentinel's HP. Cutting one down is a strike: the one turn in the game you spend without moving. That single pause flips your parity, and later makes your echo stand still — the only way a plate is ever held twice running, and the only way a gate stays open instead of blinking.

Later levels also take away pacing on the spot: brittle floor collapses the moment you step off it, and a NO BACKTRACK level forbids returning to last turn's tile. Killing time has to be a real loop around something.

Legend

  • YOU — the present
  • ECHO — you, N turns ago. Corner ticks mark where it steps next.
  • PLATE — gates open while every plate is held down, which is never longer than one turn
  • GATE
  • SHARD — the exit stays sealed until you carry every one. Echoes walk straight over them; only the present picks things up.
  • SENTINEL — blocks its tile, and carries HP. STRIKE (space) cuts down every adjacent sentinel you outmatch and spends the turn standing still. POWER = 1 + shards held, so a 2 HP guard costs you one shard first. Grey means you are not strong enough yet — and then no swing is offered at all, so there is no free pause in it either.
  • BRITTLE — one crossing only. Step off it and it collapses. Striking does not break it, because a strike never leaves the tile.
  • EXIT — barred while a shard is still out there

The rule that matters: you cannot wait, so a gate blinks for exactly one turn per visit. Two gates in a row means going back to the plate twice, at exactly the right spacing.

The exception: strike while standing on a plate, and the echo replaying that turn stands on it twice running — the gate stays open for two turns instead of one. One swing clears every sentinel around you, so each tile is worth exactly one pause.

On levels flagged NO BACKTRACK you may not step back onto the tile you stood on last turn — a red bar marks it. Burning a turn then means walking a real loop, not hopping on the spot. Striking clears the bar, because on that turn you never left.
